## Summary

In this episode of Tech Health Today, host David Chen talks with Dr. Priya Patel and Alex Moore about whether AI in healthcare is hype or reality. Chen opens by saying AI is everywhere but complicated in medicine, and he sets out to separate real uses from inflated expectations.

Dr. Patel confirms that diagnostic and triage assistants are already in use. Some hospitals use AI to read X-rays or flag urgent cases, but she stresses that these tools assist clinicians and do not replace them. Moore adds that regulatory barriers are significant. He says the FDA reviews these systems carefully, so fully autonomous AI is unlikely soon. Asked what patients might see directly, Patel points to smart triage chatbots on hospital websites and apps that help patients decide their next steps before seeing a doctor.

Moore names data privacy and bias as major hurdles, because AI learns from existing clinical data that may be skewed. Chen asks why adoption keeps growing anyway. Patel cites efficiency and clinician support, since hospitals want faster reads and fewer errors. Moore says the COVID-19 pandemic sped up digital health adoption, including AI tools.

On the future, Patel expects AI copilots that suggest treatment plans, with a human always making the final decision. Moore says interoperability is essential and that AI will work best when systems exchange data securely. Chen closes by recommending cautious optimism: AI is powerful, but its role in healthcare should stay assistive and well regulated.
